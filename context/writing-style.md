# Writing style for every artefact

These rules apply to every agent. The goal is that a stakeholder reads the output and doesn't think "AI wrote this".

## Language
- British English: prioritise, organisation, behaviour, programme, licence (noun), analyse.
- Dates as 20 Nov 2026. Currency as £1,200. Percentages as 12%.

## Tone
- Plain and direct. Write like a PO talking to their team, not like a brochure.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Say who does what: "Credit Risk confirms the threshold", not "the threshold will be confirmed".
- State uncertainty honestly: "we think", "we don't know yet", "assumed, not checked".

## Avoid
- Filler and hype: leverage, seamless, robust, cutting-edge, game-changer, unlock, empower, delve, holistic, synergy, best-in-class, world-class, elevate, streamline (unless literal), "in today's fast-paced world".
- Tricolons for rhythm ("fast, simple and secure") unless all three are real requirements.
- Em-dash-heavy sentences. Use a full stop or a comma.
- Headings that are questions. Bold on every other phrase.
- Closing summaries that repeat the document.

## Evidence
- Every number has a source file or is labelled **Assumed**.
- Quotes from customers are short and attributed to a source file and participant ID, never invented.
- If the evidence doesn't support a claim, don't make it. Put it under "Open questions".

## Markers
- `[[OPEN]]` marks something the PO must decide. `/approve` warns if any are left.
- `TODO` marks something the agent couldn't complete.
